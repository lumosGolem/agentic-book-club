## Chapter 4

### Scene I — Friar Lawrence’s Cell

Paris meets Friar Lawrence and speaks of his upcoming marriage to Juliet. Juliet arrives and speaks evasively to Paris, avoiding direct commitment. After Paris leaves, Juliet fully reveals her desperation to Friar Lawrence, threatening suicide rather than marrying Paris.

Friar Lawrence presents a plan: Juliet will drink a potion that induces a death-like sleep lasting 42 hours. She will appear dead, be placed in the Capulet tomb, and later awaken when Romeo arrives to take her away. Juliet accepts the plan without hesitation, demonstrating her resolve and willingness to embrace extreme measures.

***

### Scene II — Hall in Capulet’s House

Capulet is preparing the wedding arrangements. Juliet returns and outwardly submits to her father, apologising for her earlier defiance and agreeing to marry Paris. Capulet, pleased by her obedience, moves the wedding forward to Wednesday.

Juliet’s compliance is strategic, allowing her to carry out Friar Lawrence’s plan. The acceleration of the wedding heightens urgency and reduces the margin for error.

***

### Scene III — Juliet’s Chamber

Alone, Juliet prepares to drink the potion. She experiences intense fear and doubt, imagining possible outcomes: the potion might fail, might kill her, or she might awaken in the tomb among corpses, leading to madness or suicide.

Despite these visions, Juliet chooses action and drinks the potion. This scene emphasises her psychological struggle and courage, marking her full commitment to the plan.

***

### Scene IV — Hall in Capulet’s House

The Capulet household is busy with last-minute wedding preparations. Capulet directs servants and oversees arrangements. The Nurse goes to wake Juliet, maintaining the expectation that the marriage will proceed.

This scene builds dramatic irony, as the audience knows Juliet’s true condition while the household anticipates celebration.

***

### Scene V — Juliet’s Chamber; Juliet on the bed

The Nurse discovers Juliet apparently dead and raises the alarm. Lady Capulet and Capulet mourn her deeply, their grief immediate and overwhelming.

Paris arrives and joins in lamentation. Friar Lawrence urges that Juliet be taken to the family tomb without delay, reinforcing the plan while disguising its intent.

The wedding preparations transform into funeral rites. The scene underscores the fragility of life and the abrupt shift from joy to despair, advancing the tragic progression.

***

**Overall progression of Chapter IV:**

* Introduces and commits to the central deception (the potion)
* Establishes Juliet’s full independence and readiness to face death
* Accelerates timing, increasing risk and limiting corrective options
* Intensifies dramatic irony through the staged death
* Transitions from anticipated marriage to apparent death, propelling the tragedy toward its final outcome
